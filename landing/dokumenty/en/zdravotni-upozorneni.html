BlooMia is not a medical device

BlooMia is not a medical device within the meaning of medical device legislation. It is not intended for the diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis or treatment of any disease or medical condition.

It is an app for information and for keeping records. It helps you see where you are on your journey, what you have written down, and what may be worth reading today.

The content is educational, not individual advice

The articles, daily cards, checklists and glossary are general. They describe what usually happens during treatment, not what is happening to you.

Nothing in the app constitutes individual medical advice, a medical opinion or a position on your particular situation.

Two women the same number of days after transfer can have completely different findings, a different protocol and different instructions from their clinic. The app knows none of that. It only knows what you have written into it yourself.

What the app does not do

  • It does not diagnose.
  • It does not predict the outcome of IVF and does not calculate your individual chance of pregnancy.
  • It does not judge lab values as good or bad. It can plot them over time, but interpreting them is the doctor's job.
  • It does not set or change medication doses. The medication and doses you record are your own notes based on your clinic's instructions, not instructions from the app.
  • It does not judge how urgent your situation is.
  • It does not replace a doctor, a clinic or medical care.
  • It does not decide for you and does not urge you to carry on or to stop.
  • It does not use a chatbot in place of professional judgement.

The symptoms you record are not evidence. They can come from the hormone treatment, from ordinary changes in your body, or from pregnancy. On their own, symptoms are not a reliable way to tell how a transfer turned out, neither when there are many of them nor when there are none.

Where what you see comes from

The app selects content from the stage and the day you entered yourself, and from your most recent relevant records, for example your last transfer and the age of the embryo that was transferred.

That is context, not a prediction. When the app shows that you are six days after the transfer of a day-3 embryo, so that the embryo age is nine days, it is only stating what follows from your own entries. It says nothing about how the transfer turned out.

If you enter an inaccurate detail, the content will be inaccurate too. The app does not verify what you enter.

When not to wait and to call the clinic

Some daily cards point out situations where waiting until morning is usually not advised. In particular:

  • heavy bleeding,
  • severe or sudden pain,
  • signs of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS), for example a rapidly growing abdomen, shortness of breath, markedly reduced urination,
  • fever,
  • an acute mental health crisis.

This list is not exhaustive and is not a way to assess your condition. If anything is acute or unclear, contact your clinic, your doctor, or in an emergency the emergency medical service. In the Czech Republic that is 155; the single European emergency number 112 works across the EU.

When things get heavy

Fertility treatment is not only a medical matter. If you need to talk right now, 116 123 is the free, round-the-clock, anonymous emotional support line in the Czech Republic. Outside the Czech Republic, look up your local crisis line.

If you are thinking about harming yourself, please seek help immediately. An app is not the place for that and never will be.

The decisions stay yours and your doctor's

For decisions about your treatment, what your treating doctor or clinic says always applies. Never change your treatment, your doses or your appointments on the basis of something you read in the app.
